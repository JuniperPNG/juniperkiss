Whilst applying for PhDs and polishing my CV, I realised that although the
research jobs, grants and awards look very impressive, I had great stories behind
all of those bullet points — chance meetings, filling out applications while
living in a tent, random emails, and amazing people.

The stories include how I ended up studying Plant Biology after studying Marine
Biology part-time for three years, how I raised £40,000 from grants and awards,
how I lived with farmers in Nepal, and how I developed a passion for invasive
brambles.

I also try to explain why I once had 70 fish in a suitcase and two sharks on my
back on a London tube, and why I was in a dark forest with metre-long shears and
red stains on my clothes.

Behind the fun and unusual stories, I do confess the sacrifices, and how world
events affect a future scientist's motivation. I share my
[university assignments with marks](/assignments/) so that international students,
UK students and educators can see what an essay receiving over 90% in the UK
system looks like.

## Who this is for

- Undergraduates who either do, or do not, know where they want to end up.
- Graduate students who need a reminder to have a laugh when experiments or
  presentations do not go according to plan.
- Professors and researchers who are now teaching, supervising and employing my
  generation, and the generations of scientists after it.
